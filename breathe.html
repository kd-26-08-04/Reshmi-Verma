Clinical Diagnostic Test

The Body Oxygen Level Test (BOLT)

Your BOLT score is the gold-standard measurement of functional carbon dioxide tolerance and physiological airway resilience developed by Patrick McKeown (Oxygen Advantage).

Standardized Testing Protocol:

  1. Sit comfortably upright in a quiet chair. Rest for 2 minutes.
  2. Take a normal, silent, gentle breath in through your nose (2–3 seconds).
  3. Allow a natural, silent breath out through your nose.
  4. Pinch your nose with your fingers and immediately tap "Start BOLT Hold" below.
  5. Hold until you feel the first definite desire to breathe (an involuntary swallow or twitch of the diaphragm).
  6. Tap "Tap At First Urge" immediately!
CRITICAL CLINICAL RULE: This is NOT a breath-holding contest or endurance challenge. Your first breath after release MUST be completely calm, silent, and through your nose. If you gasp or take a deep gulp of air, you held too long and the score is invalid!

Respiratory Biochemistry

The Medical Science of Breathwork

Why conventional deep breathing advice can actually deplete cellular oxygen, and how carbon dioxide recalibration changes everything.

01

The Bohr Effect

Oxygen in the bloodstream binds to hemoglobin. It cannot be released into tissues, the brain, or muscles without adequate carbon dioxide. Over-breathing removes CO₂, locking oxygen onto hemoglobin and paradoxically suffocating cells.

02

Chemoreceptor Pacing

Your respiratory urge is not triggered by a lack of oxygen—it is triggered by sensitive chemoreceptors in your brainstem detecting CO₂ build-up. Regular post-exhale breath holds train these receptors to tolerate higher CO₂, silencing anxiety loops.

03

Vagal Efferent Firing

Extended slow exhalations (such as 4-7-8 or 5-5 cadence) stimulate the pulmonary stretch receptors. This activates the vagus nerve brake, releasing acetylcholine to instantly lower heart rate, blood pressure, and cortisol.
